GAI in Higher Education: A Skewed Landscape

  • Most GAI-in-education research centers student use, perceptions, and outcomes
  • We know far less about how instructors reason through the tensions GAI introduces
  • What drives instructor decision-making?
  • How do instructors update their understanding over time?

This presentation zooms in on that last question

By surfacing instructors’ “aha” moments, we’re watching their understanding evolve

The Study

Study Details

  • Semi-structured interviews with engineering instructors
  • Part of a larger NSF CAREER project (VT IRB #24-572)
  • Explored how STEM engineering instructors think about GAI for teaching, learning, and assessment

Noticed recurring “edge of understanding” moments at various interview points where instructors encountered new possibilities and tensions around GAI, requiring pedagogical reasoning in real time

“Aha” Moments: Windows into Instructor Reasoning

Self-Generated Example Guides Evaluation

Question: Have you considered requesting that students submit the prompts they use or the conversations they have with GAI as part of their assignments?

Response I mean, that’s a good question. I hadn’t thought that through, but perhaps that would make sense, yeah. I don’t know how much. I guess it comes down to what are we trying to teach, right? If I was trying to teach how to do a report, then you would have to be a lot more granular in understanding what they’re doing themselves and what they’re doing with GAI. If I’m trying to see if I want them to generate output that is professional looking, and we’ve already taught them how to write a report – because I think that those are two different things – maybe it’s enough to say that GAI was using the generation of this.

Potential Future Applications

Question: Have you ever considered using GAI systems to predict student performance in a class or on a project?

Response: Uh, I hadn’t considered that, but if there’s a way I could use it to see if students are at risk – kind of as a retention tool – that would be interesting. Maybe in the LMS a way to predict how frequently [a student is] logging in and their grades, and [the GAI system] could alert me, rather than doing that manually. I do that in our Canvas LMS when students score below a certain threshold. I’ll have Canvas send them an email reaching out to them. But yeah, if there’s a way that [GAI] can do some of that for me, that would be nice. So I would, but I just haven’t really thought about it. I’d want to know more about the tool. If there was a tool that would do that, I’d be interested in looking at it.

Hypothesis: Divergence suggests the need for structured “backstage” spaces for reflection, and not reflections of instructors’ “AI literacy”

Implications

Why These “Aha” Moments Matter

  • “Aha” moments are windows into pedagogical reasoning that educators and researchers can learn from and build on
    • John Loughran (2019): Teachers are often too busy doing teaching to unpack the reasoning behind their practice
  • Without structured reflection, these moments of potential may not catalyze into durable change
    • Jennifer Turns & Yuliana Flores (2025): Adopting GAI into existing work practices is an emotionally laden process that unfolds through multiplecritical moments of “Can I?” vs. “Should I?” reasoning


Loughran, J. (2019). Pedagogical reasoning: The foundation of the professional knowledge of teaching. Teachers and Teaching | Turns, J. A., & Flores, Y. (2025, June 22). Generative AI as a Thinking Partner in Doctoral Education: An Autoethnographic Exploration. 2025 ASEE Annual Conference & Exposition

The Problem

  • Participants rarely mentioned existing mechanisms at their institutions for reflecting on GAI integration, and when they did, they were often dismissive of them
    • Too simple and generic
    • More focused on “AI literacy” than on pedagogical reasoning and decision-making
    • Lecture-style instruction vs. structured, low-stakes conversations with colleagues
  • The research protocol was sufficient to spark these episodes

Path A: Supported Reflection

Growth, curiosity, and connection with knowledgeable colleagues

Path B: No Structure

Intellectual retreat and disengagement from the possibilities of GAI for teaching, assessment, and learning

What might intentional, sustained efforts do? Communities of practice with differentiated support for instructors at different levels of GAI integration? Designated reflection systems for institutions?

Wenger, E. (1998). Communities of practice: Learning, meaning, and identity

Takeaways & Future Work

We Need More Research On…

  • Instructor reasoning, experiences, norms, and decision-making around GAI
  • Designated, structured moments for individual and collective reflection
    • Examples: CETL focus groups, self-paced reflections, communities of practice, department-level discussions

Session theme: The reflective practices we value for students are equally vital for instructors navigating GAI.

Future Work

  • What institutional mechanisms best support these moments?
  • How do they lead to durable expertise network formations over time?
  • Instructors need reflection spaces too

It is insufficient to simply encourage instructors to “reflect” without connecting that reflection back to practice. Additional research is needed to understand how to design and sustain these spaces for reflection, and how they can be connected to practice in ways that support durable change.
